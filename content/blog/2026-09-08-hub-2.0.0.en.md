## What's new in Cryptomator Hub 2.0.0?

Two areas are in focus: **User & Group Management** and **Emergency Access**.

### User & Group Management: Manage users and groups directly in Hub

Until now, managing people and groups meant going through Keycloak. With version 2.0.0, it happens **directly in the Cryptomator Hub interface** — one tool switch less in your daily work.

- Create and manage users and groups directly in Hub
- Grant permissions via groups instead of maintaining every access individually
- Less administrative effort and less setup complexity

An example: in a small business, accounting needs access to financial records, HR to personnel records, and project teams to their respective working documents. Groups let you map structures like these instead of authorizing every person individually. New team members join the right group and get exactly the permissions they need. If someone changes roles or leaves the team, you adjust the group membership — done.

This helps small and medium-sized teams in particular, who don't have dedicated IAM expertise in-house.

### Emergency Access: Restore access when it matters

What happens when the person who owns a vault is on vacation, off sick, or no longer with the company?

**Emergency Access** answers that without weakening the security model:

- Admins define a group of trusted people.
- Using multi-party approval, this group can restore access to a vault.
- Recovery therefore always involves several people — no single person can grant themselves access.

## What is Cryptomator Hub?

Cryptomator Hub is our **solution for encrypted collaboration within teams**. While the classic Cryptomator app is designed to let individuals encrypt their files client-side and store them securely in the cloud, Hub brings this principle to teams and organizations.

You create encrypted vaults for your teams, manage access rights centrally, and keep using the cloud storage of your choice: OneDrive, Google Drive, Dropbox, Nextcloud, or any other provider. The files stay wherever you store them — **Hub takes care of access control and key management**, but never becomes the storage location for your data itself.

The Cryptomator app remains the application you use to open and work with your vaults locally. Hub doesn't replace it; it extends it with what teams additionally need. The core principle stays the same: **your data is encrypted client-side**. Neither cloud providers nor unauthorized people can read your files in plaintext.

## Why not just use the Cryptomator app in a team?

The classic Cryptomator app **is ideal for individuals**. In principle, a vault can be used by several people, too — but in practice that means a password has to be shared.

And shared passwords are always problematic in teams: they get passed on through chats or password managers without it staying clear who actually still knows them. They're nearly impossible to revoke. If someone should no longer have access, removing them from a group isn't enough — the password would have to be changed and redistributed to everyone who's authorized.

On top of that come questions that a commonly known password simply can't answer:

- Who is allowed to access which vault?
- How do you add new people and reliably lock out those who leave?
- How do you keep administration manageable when multiple teams, departments, or projects are involved?
- What happens when a responsible person no longer has access?

That's why Cryptomator Hub organizes access not through a shared password, but through **individual accounts, roles, and permissions**. Each person works with their own access, and access can be granted and revoked in a targeted way.

## Who actually holds the keys?

Confidential information — customer data, contracts, financial and HR records, internal strategy documents — mostly lives in the cloud today. That's convenient, because teams can work flexibly and collaborate across locations. At the same time, the question arises: **who can actually see this data?**

Cloud providers do have their own security mechanisms, but not every kind of encryption is the same. In many cases, data is stored encrypted, but **the keys are held by the provider itself**. Technically, it has the ability to decrypt the content — if it wants to or is compelled to do so.

That's why Cryptomator Hub brings **client-side encryption** into the team context: **the data is encrypted before it reaches the cloud**. Only authorized people can get to the content. That makes Hub interesting for organizations that want the convenience of the cloud without giving up the confidentiality of their data.

## Why Hub is especially useful for small teams

Small teams and businesses often face a dilemma: they want to protect sensitive data, but they don't have a large IT department, complex security infrastructure, or a dedicated compliance team. That's exactly where Hub plays to its strengths.

**1. Individual access instead of shared passwords**

Every person has their own access. That pays off above all during offboarding: when someone leaves the company, access is revoked centrally — without having to reorganize a vault or redistribute a password.

**2. Structure instead of one vault for everything**

A single vault is enough for individuals. Teams usually need several areas: for management, accounting, marketing, or individual customer projects. With Hub, these become separate vaults that are shared in a targeted way. That way, each person only gets access to the data they really need — as much as necessary, as little as possible.

**3. Security that doesn't get in the way**

In small teams, security is often organized on the side. If access control is complicated, it gets bypassed. Hub makes the secure option the practical one: users log in, open their shared vaults, and work with their files — without exchanging passwords or having to understand technical details.

**4. Free choice of cloud storage**

Hub doesn't dictate which cloud storage you have to use. It adds an extra layer of security to your existing infrastructure instead of replacing it.

**5. Zero-knowledge: we can't see your data either**

Hub manages access and key material in such a way that encrypted collaboration becomes possible without any central entity — including us — getting to your unencrypted content. That's the zero-knowledge principle. And because Hub is open source, it can be verified.

The more sensitive the data in the cloud, the more important the question of who really has access to it. With Cryptomator Hub, teams keep that control — and with version 2.0.0, administration becomes considerably easier: the new User & Group Management brings people, groups, and permissions together in one place, and Emergency Access makes sure nobody stays locked out when a responsible person is unavailable.

For individuals, the classic Cryptomator app remains the right choice. For teams, small businesses, and organizations, Hub is the next logical step.
